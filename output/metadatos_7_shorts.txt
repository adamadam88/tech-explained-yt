1. why-do-phone-batteries-get-worse-over-time
======================================================================

TÍTULO:
The Real Reason Your Battery Gets Worse 🔋 #shorts

DESCRIPCIÓN:
Your battery isn't just 'old': lithium gets trapped in a growing SEI layer on the graphite and never comes back.
Heat and sitting at 100% speed it up. Apple rates ~80% capacity after 500 cycles (1,000 on iPhone 15).

Sources:
- Apple – Maximizing Battery Life and Lifespan (above 35 °C can permanently damage capacity): https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/
- AppleInsider – iPhone 15 battery rated for 1,000 cycles; iPhone 14 and earlier, 500: https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/02/20/apple-says-the-iphone-15-battery-is-more-durable-than-it-originally-thought
- Battery Design – SEI growth (irreversible lithium loss, faster at high temperature and high state of charge): https://www.batterydesign.net/chemistry/solid-electrolyte-interphase/sei-growth/
- Ionworks – Batteries 101: degradation (loss of lithium inventory, heat, high SOC): https://docs.ionworks.com/guide/batteries-101/degradation

2. how-does-wi-fi-go-through-walls
======================================================================

TÍTULO:
Why Wi-Fi Goes Through Walls (But Not Metal) 📶 #shorts

DESCRIPCIÓN:
Wi-Fi is light you can't see, with waves about 12 cm long — drywall and wood barely slow it down.
Concrete soaks it up, metal reflects it, and water (including you) absorbs it.

Sources:
- NASA Imagine the Universe – The electromagnetic spectrum (radio, microwaves and light are the same radiation): https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/toolbox/emspectrum1.html
- NASA Earthdata – Electromagnetic spectrum (visible light ≈ 380–700 nm): https://earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/earth-observation-data-basics/electromagnetic-spectrum
- Interline – Building materials and Wi-Fi signal loss at 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz (drywall 3–6 dB, brick 8–10 dB, concrete up to ~25 dB): https://interline.pl/Information-and-Tips/Building-Materials-and-Wi-Fi-Signal-Loss-2.4-GHz-and-5-GHz
- eero – How to boost Wi-Fi signals through walls (metal reflects, water absorbs): https://eero.com/blog/articles/how-to-boost-wifi-signals-through-walls
- USGS – The water in you (the human body is up to ~60% water): https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/water-you-water-and-human-body
- Sciencing – What's the mesh inside your microwave door (metal enclosure and mesh reflect the microwaves back inside): https://www.sciencing.com/1834813/whats-mesh-inside-microwave-door-function-explained

5. how-does-the-internet-cross-the-ocean
======================================================================

TÍTULO:
The Internet Is Mostly at the Bottom of the Ocean 🌊 #shorts

DESCRIPCIÓN:
Over 99% of data between continents travels through about 600 cables lying on the seafloor.
Inside: hair-thin glass fibers carrying light, steel for strength and copper powering repeaters every 50–100 km.

Sources:
- TeleGeography – congressional testimony: submarine cables carry over 99% of intercontinental data: https://resources.telegeography.com/hubfs/Tim%20Stronge%20Written%20Congressional%20Testimony%20%7C%20TeleGeography.pdf
- TeleGeography – How many submarine cables are there, anyway? (~600 systems, ~1.48 million km in 2025): https://blog.telegeography.com/how-many-submarine-cables-are-there-anyway
- ICPC – Damage to submarine cables: 150–200 faults a year, ~70–80% from accidental human activity such as fishing and anchors: https://iscpc.org/publications/icpc-viewpoints/damage-to-submarine-cables-from-dragged-anchors/
- Fibre Systems – ICPC: sharks don't dine on subsea fibre-optic cables: https://www.fibre-systems.com/news/sharks-dont-dine-subsea-fibre-optic-cables-says-icpc
- US patent 4278835 – submarine cable: optical fibres in a conductive tube, steel wires, conductor powering the repeaters: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4278835
- Hengtong – submarine fiber optic cable: repeaters every 50–100 km: https://www.hengtongglobal.com/info/submarine-fiber-optic-cable-103155332.html

7. why-does-your-phone-get-hot
======================================================================

TÍTULO:
Your Phone Is Secretly a Tiny Heater 🔥 #shorts

DESCRIPCIÓN:
Almost all the power your phone's chip uses ends up as heat — and phones have no fan to blow it away.
Graphite sheets and vapor chambers spread it into the case; if that's not enough, the phone slows itself down.

Sources:
- Android Central – Tech Talk: why does my phone get hot?: https://www.androidcentral.com/phones/tech-talk-why-does-my-phone-get-hot
- BGR – Why smartphones don't have built-in fans (passive cooling, graphite sheets, vapor chambers): https://www.bgr.com/2193362/smartphones-cooling-explained-no-fans/
- CG Director – CPU TDP explained (power a processor consumes is exhausted as heat): https://www.cgdirector.com/cpu-tdp-thermal-design-power-explained
- SamMobile – Why is my phone getting hot? (thermal throttling): https://www.sammobile.com/news/why-is-my-phone-getting-hot-and-should-i-worry/
